ADR-0007: Replace the Ferrite storage engine with Halyard
Meridian Systems - Corvus Platform - Status: Accepted, 2031-08-22
Deciders: Corvus platform team, with sign-off from the Meridian architecture council

Context

Corvus originally stored ingested events in Ferrite, a log-structured merge tree written for
the first version of the platform. Ferrite compacted continuously, which meant that write
amplification grew with retention: a Platinum tenant holding 400 days of data paid for the
same bytes to be rewritten roughly eleven times over the life of a segment. Compaction also
competed with ingest for disk bandwidth, so the worst ingest latency of the day reliably
coincided with the largest compaction.

Two further problems drove the decision. Ferrite's segment index was global rather than
per-segment, so recovery after a node loss required replaying the whole index, which took
over 40 minutes on a full node. And Ferrite could not seal a segment without a quiescent
period, which made an online migration of any kind expensive.

Decision

We replace Ferrite with Halyard, an append-only segment store with no background compaction.
Segments are written once, sealed at 512 MB or 15 minutes, and never rewritten. Each sealed
segment carries its own index, so recovery loads only the segments a query touches.

Alternatives considered

Keep Ferrite and tune compaction. Rejected: tuning moves the cost around but does not remove
write amplification, and the recovery time is a property of the global index, not of the
compaction schedule.

Tier cold data to object storage behind Ferrite, an option the team called Obsidian.
Rejected for now: it addresses storage cost but not ingest latency or recovery time, and it
adds a second failure domain to the read path. Obsidian remains a candidate for a later
cost-reduction effort and is explicitly not cancelled by this decision.

Adopt a third-party time-series engine. Rejected: none of the three engines evaluated
supported the per-tenant isolation model Corvus requires, and adopting one would move the
platform's core availability guarantee outside Meridian's control.

Consequences

Storage footprint grows by an estimated 20 to 30 percent because Halyard never compacts away
superseded events; this is accepted as the price of predictable ingest latency.
There is no secondary index on arbitrary fields, so queries that filter on a non-indexed
field must scan the segments in their timespan. This is why per-tier query timespan limits
exist.
Recovery after node loss is bounded by the segments a query touches rather than by total
retention.
The migration is online and is delivered in release 4.3.
