The Solar System

The Sun is the star at the center of the Solar System. It contains about 99.86 percent of
the system's total mass. Energy is produced in the Sun's core through nuclear fusion, where
hydrogen nuclei fuse into helium.

Jupiter is the largest planet in the Solar System. It is a gas giant with a mass more than
two and a half times that of all the other planets combined. Jupiter's Great Red Spot is a
giant storm that has persisted for centuries.

Mars is often called the Red Planet because of the iron oxide on its surface. It has the
tallest volcano in the Solar System, Olympus Mons, which stands about 22 kilometers high.

Saturn is famous for its prominent ring system, made mostly of ice particles with smaller
amounts of rocky debris and dust.
